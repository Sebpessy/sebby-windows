How to budget window replacement in Dallas–Fort Worth

By Sebastien Pessy, owner of Sebby Windows · Updated August 2026

Windows are the home upgrade people put off longest, and it is almost never because they do not want them. It is the number at the bottom of the quote. Financing, on approved credit, changes that question from can I write this check to can I carry this payment, and for most DFW households those are very different questions.

Five questions to ask before you sign anything

Home-improvement financing terms are not all the same, and the differences are where the money hides. Ask these five, of us or of anyone else, and get the answers in writing:

  • Is it a real rate, or deferred interest? Many home-improvement promotions are structured as "no interest if paid in full," where missing the deadline triggers every month of back interest at once. A true 0% promotion never does that. These are very different products with very similar advertising.
  • What is the APR, and for how many months? Written down, with the monthly payment that goes with it.
  • Is there a prepayment penalty? If you clear the balance in month eight, you want to have paid exactly the amount on your quote, not a dollar more.
  • When does the money actually fund? On a well-run job the financed amount disburses when the work is finished and you have walked every opening.
  • Are there dealer fees priced into the job? Some contractors buy down a promotional rate and quietly add the cost back into the quote.

Sebby Windows offers financing through a third-party lender, on approved credit. Approval, rate, and term are the lender's decision, not ours, and you get their terms in writing before you commit. Financing is optional and it does not change your price: we do not price a cash discount in, so paying by check costs exactly what financing costs.

Why financing changes the right scope of work

Cost per window drops as the count rises. Mobilization, permitting, disposal, and travel are spread across every opening on the job, so twelve windows at once always beats four windows three times over. Homeowners paying cash usually know this and phase anyway, because the lump sum for a whole house is the obstacle.

Financed, the arithmetic flips. Doing the whole house at once costs less per window, gets the west and south elevations fixed in the same summer as everything else, and spreads across the term instead of landing in one month. Phasing is paying more per window for the privilege of waiting.

Why we do not publish a per-window price

Because an honest one does not exist until somebody measures your openings. Two houses on the same street can differ by a wide margin on the same window count. What actually sets your number:

Size and style

A picture window costs less per square foot than a double-hung of the same size, since there is no moving hardware. But an oversized unit needs two installers and sometimes a lift, which costs more in labor than it saved in hardware. Sliding patio doors price separately.

The glass package

Low-E coating, argon fill, and a low solar heat gain coefficient add real cost and are worth it on west and south walls in this climate. Tempered glass is required by code near doors, tubs, showers, and stair landings, and adds cost to those openings specifically.

What is behind the trim

This is the one that surprises people. Brick, stucco, and siding each detail differently. A rotted sill, a failed lintel, or a previous retrofit installed badly changes the work. A full-frame replacement, where the old frame comes out down to the rough opening, is meaningfully more than an insert. We find that at the measure, not on install day, and it appears as its own visible line.

How many you do at once

See above. This is the single largest lever you control.

How to read any quote you are handed

Ask for these four things in writing, from us or anyone else. A company unwilling to put them on paper is telling you something:

  • A price per opening, not one bundled whole-house number. Bundling is how an inexpensive window and an expensive one get averaged into a figure you cannot evaluate, and it is how change orders get justified later.
  • The product line and series name, not just "vinyl double-pane."
  • The glass specs, U-factor and SHGC, for the units being installed.
  • The warranty terms, product and labor, stated separately.

And if there is financing attached, work back through the five questions above, starting with whether the promotion is a true rate or deferred interest.

About the tax credit

The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit under Section 25C, which covered a portion of qualifying window costs, expired for property placed in service after December 31, 2025 under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. A number of DFW contractors are still advertising it. If a quote leans on that credit to justify its price in 2026, the math behind that quote is wrong. Confirm your own situation with your CPA.

Is it worth doing at all?

On an older DFW home with single-pane aluminum, yes, and not mainly for the energy bill. The comfort change is immediate: west rooms stop being unusable at 4pm, the AC cycles instead of running flat out through August, and street noise drops noticeably. Energy savings are real but modest, typically 10 to 15 percent of the cooling portion of your bill. Buy windows for comfort and for keeping water out of your walls, and treat the utility savings as a bonus.
