02 · What You're Paying For

Water doesn't come through the glass. It comes around it.

A window is a hole cut in your weather barrier. What decides whether that hole leaks in year seven is the six inches of detail around the frame, and that detail is invisible the moment the trim goes back on.

Most retrofit crews skip the sill pan entirely, because once the stool and casing go back on, nobody can tell. We pan every opening, lap every flashing so water runs outward, and seal and insulate the perimeter on both sides.

01

Full tear-out

Old sash, frame, and fasteners come out. We inspect the sill and framing for rot before anything new goes in. A shell over a rotted sill hides the problem and doubles the cost later.

02

Sill pan, back dam

A sloped pan with sealed end dams and a back dam sits under the unit. If water ever gets past the frame, it drains outward instead of into your wall.

03

Flashing in order

Jamb tape laps over the sill pan. Head flashing tucks under the weather barrier. Getting that order backwards is the most common defect we find on other work.

04

Plumb, level, shimmed

Shimmed at the load points so the frame is not racked. A racked frame binds the sash, and binding sash is what kills seals and warranties.

05

Sealed both sides

Low-expansion foam at the perimeter, backer rod and a proper exterior sealant joint, interior air seal. Not a bead of caulk over a gap.

06

Trim, stool, clean

Casing and stool restored, blinds rehung, glass cleaned, old units hauled off. You should not be able to tell we were there, except through the window.

03 · Glass for Texas

In this climate, SHGC matters more than U-factor.

In Minnesota you buy a window to keep heat in, and U-factor rules. In North Texas you buy a window to keep the sun's heat out, and the number that does that is SHGC, the solar heat gain coefficient. Plenty of salesmen quote a great U-factor and never mention it.

Still on aluminum?

A huge share of DFW homes built before the mid-1990s still carry single-pane aluminum. The frame itself is a heat bridge: it conducts summer heat straight into the room no matter what the glass does. That is why those rooms are hot at 4pm whatever the thermostat says, and why the west side of the house is always the worst.

Aluminum to Low-E vinyl is the single largest comfort change available on an older DFW home.

After a hailstorm.

Hail an inch or larger cracks glass, dents frames, and breaks insulated-glass seals. A seal failure often shows up weeks later as fog between the panes that you cannot wipe off. Most Texas homeowners policies cover hail and wind-driven debris damage to windows, subject to your deductible.

We photograph every damaged opening and write a scope your adjuster can price from, and we will tell you plainly if we think you do not have a claim.

05 · Your Quote

What your quote is built from.

We do not publish a per-window price, because an honest one does not exist until somebody measures your openings. Here is what actually moves the number, so you can read any quote you are handed, ours included.

01

Size and Style

A picture window costs less per square foot than a double-hung of the same size, because there is no moving hardware. An oversized unit needs two installers and sometimes a lift. Sliders and patio doors price on their own.

02

Glass Package

Low-E coating, argon fill, and a low SHGC add real cost and are worth it on west and south walls. Tempered glass is code-required near doors, tubs, and stair landings, and adds to those specific openings.

03

What's Behind the Trim

Brick, stucco, and siding each detail differently. Rotted sills, failed lintels, or a bad previous retrofit change the scope. We find that at the measure, not on install day, and price it as its own visible line.

Every opening is quoted individually, in writing, with the product line and glass package named. We do not hand out a single bundled whole-house number, because bundling is how an inexpensive window and an expensive one get averaged into a figure you cannot evaluate.

08 · Questions

Answered straight.

Do you offer financing?

Yes, through a third-party lender, on approved credit. The rate, the term, and the monthly payment are set by the lender based on your credit, not by us, and you get their terms in writing before you sign anything. Approval takes a few minutes and can be done before the in-home measure. Financing is optional and paying by check or card costs exactly the same.

How much does window replacement cost in Dallas–Fort Worth?

It depends on the size and style of each opening, the frame material, the glass package, and what condition the framing is in behind the trim. An honest per-window number does not exist until somebody measures, which is why we quote every opening individually in writing with the product line and glass package named. The quote is free and there is no presentation attached to it.

How long does it take?

Installation on a typical DFW house is one to two days once the windows arrive. The wait is manufacturing: custom-sized units generally run three to six weeks from the day the order is released. Each opening is out of the wall for well under an hour, so the house is never open overnight.

Do I need a permit or HOA approval?

Usually both. Most DFW cities require a permit for window replacement, including like-for-like swaps. In an HOA, written architectural approval normally has to come before the city permit, especially when the frame color, grid pattern, or glass tint changes. We prepare the HOA package and pull the permit while your windows are in production, so it does not add time.

Is there still a federal tax credit for windows?

No. The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit under Section 25C, worth up to $600 a year for qualifying windows, expired for property placed in service after December 31, 2025 under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. What still lowers your bill is the glass: a low-SHGC Low-E package on west and south exposures. Confirm your own tax situation with your CPA.

Will insurance cover my hail-damaged windows?

Usually yes. Most Texas homeowners policies cover window damage from hail, wind, or wind-driven debris, subject to your deductible. Fogging between the panes that you cannot wipe off is a failed seal and is claimable. We photograph each damaged opening and write a scope your adjuster can price from, and we will tell you honestly when we do not think you have a claim.

What windows work best in Texas heat?

A double-pane unit with a Low-E coating and argon fill, in a vinyl or fiberglass frame. Target an SHGC of about 0.25 or lower on west and south exposures and a U-factor of 0.30 or lower. Avoid aluminum frames: the metal conducts heat straight through regardless of how good the glass is.

Vinyl or fiberglass?

Vinyl is the right answer for most DFW homes. It does not conduct heat, never needs painting, and a good vinyl window massively outperforms the aluminum it replaces. Fiberglass is stronger, holds dark exterior colors better under Texas sun, and spans larger openings, at a meaningful premium.

Can I replace only part of the house?

Yes. Start with the west and south elevations, which carry the worst heat load, then finish the rest later. Worth knowing: cost per window improves at higher counts, since mobilization, permitting, and disposal spread across more openings.

What warranty do I get?

The manufacturers we install carry a limited lifetime warranty on frame, sash, and insulated glass unit, transferable to the next owner on most product lines. Labor and installation are warrantied separately by us. The exact terms of both are written into your quote before you sign anything.

Who shows up to my house?

Sebastien Pessy, the owner, measures every job himself and is on site during installation. There is no commissioned salesman. Sebby Windows is fully insured, and a certificate of insurance is available on request before any work starts.
